Held back for safety

These steps are blocked right now to protect the pump. Each one clears by itself once it is safe — you don't have to do anything.

Common jobs

Each button runs the whole sequence in the right order, so you don't have to remember which switch comes first. You'll be asked to confirm before anything moves.

Rough down first

Runs the backing pump on its own to empty the line, without spinning the turbopump. This is normally the first thing you do.

  1. Turn the turbopump motor off
  2. Switch the station on

Spin up the turbopump

Once the line is roughed down, this lets the turbopump start turning. It takes several minutes to reach full speed.

  1. Switch the station on
  2. Turn the turbopump motor on

Shut everything down

Stops the pumps and switches off every accessory output — heating, venting, and the backing-pump relay.

  1. Motor off
  2. Heating off
  3. Venting blocked
  4. Station off, once the rotor has stopped
Never spin the turbopump up against atmosphere. Rough the line down first, or the drive will overheat and trip on its 15-minute run-up limit.

What the pump is reporting

Live values, refreshed every few seconds.

Individual controls one switch at a time

Pumping station

The master switch. Switching it on closes the relay that powers the backing pump — and starts the turbopump, unless you have turned the motor off separately.

Turbopump motor

Whether the turbopump itself is allowed to turn. Turning it off while the station is on is how you run the backing pump alone.

Standby

Runs the turbopump at a reduced speed instead of full speed. Useful when you want vacuum held but don't need full performance.

Speed setpoint

Runs the turbopump at a chosen percentage of full speed rather than flat out. Applying this also switches the drive into speed-setting mode.

Clear a fault

If the pump has reported an error and refuses to run, this clears it. If the error comes straight back, the underlying cause is still there.

Accessories heating, venting, backing pump

Casing heating (HEAT/TMS)

Warms the pump body to stop residues condensing inside it. This unit has a regulated system, so you set a target temperature rather than just switching it on.

The casing gets hot and stays hot after switching off. Let it cool before touching it.

Venting (VENT)

Lets air back into the pump when it stops. "Blocked" keeps it sealed; "released" allows it to vent according to the mode below.

Venting brings the pump back to atmospheric pressure. Don't vent while the rotor is still spinning fast.

Backing pump (FV PUMP)

The roughing pump that works below the turbopump. It has no switch of its own here — it is powered through a relay that the pumping station closes. These settings only change how it behaves once running.

Cooling fan (FAN)

There is no fan control here. The cooling unit plugs into its own connector on the drive unit and the pump runs it automatically — no setting on this bus reaches it.

Known issue on this rig. During testing the fan switched on and off with the pumping station while the backing-pump relay never clicked. That is what you would see if the FAN and FV PUMP leads were swapped at the drive unit. Worth checking the labels before trusting either output.
Connection settings

The address of the computer that is physically wired to the pump. You only need to change this if that computer's network address changes.
